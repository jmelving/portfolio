01 — Project Description

Overview

Decades Ahead is a retro car commercial inspired by the Porsche 911, placing the vehicle in a space setting that blends past and future aesthetics. Created together with Hannah Rampitsch under CrossDot Studio, the piece was produced in one month as part of our Masters degree - an open assignment set by Andreas Catucci at Panoply that we combined with our own interests in automotive design and space aesthetics. The aim was to encapsulate the timelessness of the design by offering glimpses into both decades gone and decades to come.

The narrative unfolds across three shots: an opening that reveals the car's internal wiring and signal transmission as it accelerates near a black hole; a mid-section where the vehicle tears through the Milky Way at extreme speed, leaving streaking starfields in its wake; and a final shot where the car breaks through the sound barrier as the stars fade into a timeless conclusion.

02 — Behind the Scenes

Process

My contributions spanned all shading and materials across the project, the full layout and camera work for the final shot, and compositing and rendering throughout. Hannah handled most of the particle trail effects and lighting.

§ Shading & Materials
Building the Look

The car itself is a free Singer 911 model by Szymon Kubicki on Behance, but practically every material on it had to be rebuilt from scratch for Karma - including a model for the number plate and all surface shaders. The geometry was also cleaned up and adjusted to match the specific look we were going for.

§ Final Shot Layout & Compositing
Sound Barrier & Timeless Conclusion

All compositing and rendering across the project ran through Karma and Nuke and fixed a lot of the issues that arose in 3D.

The biggest comp challenges were motion blur artifacts on simulated particles, which didn't behave cleanly in USD and needed a lot of troubleshooting and correction passes in Nuke before things came together. A significant amount of the comp work overall ended up being problem-solving rather than pure compositing.
